PIRANHA found the convoy at night and reported it to the pack; she attacked it at 0944 on 16 July (Tokyo time) and
Seattle Maru, carrying troops, sank. The convoy went on in two parts, one standing by for the rescue. That night
GUARDFISH attacked the larger part, ten ships, and THRESHER a group of six about fifty miles to the north; by 0345 on
the 17th the Japanese record counted five more ships sunk. The boats' war patrol reports beside the escort division's
record, on one GMT clock.

## Outcome

Six ships sunk, by the 1st Surface Escort Division's July diary: Seattle, Mantai, Sainei, Jinzan, Hiyama and Shozan
Maru (with their tonnages in its loss list). Its chart gives the attack from 0945 on the 16th at 19-17N 120-15E to
0345 on the 17th at 18-53N 119-44E: the first is PIRANHA's attack, within a minute, and the last THRESHER's. The
record gives no hour for the five lost that night, so which boat sank which is open; the pairings are in each boat's
files.

## The convoy

Takao to Manila, sailed on the 14th with twenty ships and (by the chart's partly legible cell) seven escorts; the
Navy's summary counts 21 transports and 8 escorts. Its own record: [[convoy:tama-21c]].

## Reading the map

Positions are each boat's own reckoning. The two parts of the convoy are the boats' observations: the diary says only
that the convoy sailed in two parts, the rescuers and the rest. Taking GUARDFISH's ten ships and THRESHER's six as
those parts is ours.

## Clocks

PIRANHA kept zone G (−7) on station, THRESHER zone H (−8), GUARDFISH zone I (−9), the same as the Japanese records
(Tokyo time).
