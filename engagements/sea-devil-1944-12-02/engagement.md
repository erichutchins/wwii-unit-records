SEA DEVIL's three attacks on a southbound convoy in the early hours of 2 December 1944, as typed on her attack
sheets, beside the Imperial Navy's summary of the night: the army transport Hawaii Maru, with 3,648 troops aboard, torpedoed
and sunk at 0430.

## Outcome

SEA DEVIL fired four bow torpedoes at 0414 I (all missed), two more at 0424 I, which she saw hit a large ship that
listed and sank rapidly (she took it for a tanker of 10,000 tons), and four stern torpedoes at 0429 I, one of which
hit a large freighter she claimed probably sunk. The Navy's summary for the day has one ship lost: Hawaii Maru, an
army-requisitioned transport of 9,500 tons carrying 3,648 troops, 50 vehicles and 60 landing craft from Moji for
Takao, torpedoed at 0430 I about 120 miles north-west of Amami-Ōshima, sunk; and at 0945 the escort CD-66 depth-charged
a submarine it had closed to 100 m (p. 2) [[cite:jacar:C16120660300]]. Six minutes after the Attack 2 hits, Hawaii
Maru is paired with that attack as probable. The summary's place is a round bearing and distance some 70 nm from
SEA DEVIL's; it names no second ship, so Attack 3's hit is unaccounted for. Secondary sources give the convoy a second
loss, Akigawa Maru; the requisitioned-ship tables' ship of that name is another, sunk in April 1944.

## Reading the map

Positions are SEA DEVIL's own; her attack sheets give the same position for all three attacks (30-51N 128-45E), and
the convoy contact at 2351 I on the 1st is typed at it too. Open circles are ships she sighted, at the target's
position.

## Clocks

SEA DEVIL kept zone I (−9) in the East China Sea, the Japanese records' zone, so the two sides' times compare as
typed.

## What is missing

The convoy's own record: the 1st Surface Escort Division's diary has no December 1944 part on JACAR, and no CD-66
record of December was found. Hawaii Maru was army-requisitioned, so she is not in the Imperial Navy's ship tables.
