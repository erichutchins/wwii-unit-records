SEAHORSE's and BANG's attacks on convoy No. 91 southwest of Pratas Island, as typed on their attack sheets, beside
the Hong Kong fleet's table of the destroyer Kuri's reports.

## Outcome

The Japanese record is the 2nd China Expeditionary Fleet's (Hong Kong) table of submarine reports for July 1944
(jacar:C08030032900 p28), from the destroyer Kuri (栗). SEAHORSE attacked first, at 2354–2355 H on 3 July (0054–0055
I), and claimed two freighters sunk; Kuri reported Nitto Maru torpedoed and sunk at 0100 I, 19-21N 115-50E. BANG
fired at 0506 I on what was left of the convoy; Kuri reported being under torpedo attack with no damage at 0515 I,
and SEAHORSE logged depth charges eleven minutes after BANG fired "(Bang?)". SEAHORSE attacked again at 1238–1239 H
and claimed two passenger freighters; Kuri reported Gyoyu Maru and No. 28 Kyodo Maru torpedoed and sunk at 1345 I,
20-18N 115-02E. The diary's narrative (p27) names it 第九一船団 (No. 91 convoy), attacked southwest of Pratas Island by
two or three submarines; its ports are not recorded there, and its course (270, then 320–335 on SEAHORSE's sheets)
points to Hong Kong. FRUPAC's "torp attack 0105/04" fits SEAHORSE's first attack.

## The convoy

Takao to Hong Kong (secondary, industrialhistoryhk:29957); SEAHORSE's course fits. SEAHORSE saw five freighters with
two destroyers and other escorts; BANG saw one AK. The convoy's own record: [[convoy:no-91-1944-07]].

## Reading the map

Positions are BANG's own dead reckoning and star sights; lines between them are straight, and where she went in
between is not recorded. Open circles are ships she sighted, at the target's position. As the clock passes each
firing time a red ring pulses at BANG's position; hover a diamond for the attack sheet.

## Clocks

BANG kept zone K (−10) for Attack 1 and zone I (−9) for Attacks 2 and 3, as her report does; GROWLER kept I on
station, but SEAHORSE kept H (−8). Japanese records use Tokyo time (I, −9): the escort division's chart has HI-67's
two oilers hit at 1515, seven minutes after BANG's sheet.

## What is missing

No Japanese record has been found yet for Attacks 1 and 3; those actions show only the American side. Where the
Japanese record names only the ships that were hit, the rest of the convoy is described as the boats saw it.
