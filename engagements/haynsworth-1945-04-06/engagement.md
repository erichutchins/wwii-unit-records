The kamikaze hit on USS Haynsworth, on picket station 12 miles from Task Group 58.3's carriers off Okinawa, on the
first day of Japan's mass kamikaze offensive. Her own reports beside the other ships of the group and
the Japanese records of the planes sent against them.

The ship's track for April 1945 is in [`ships/dd-700-haynsworth/`](../../ships/dd-700-haynsworth/README.md). The crew
lists stay out of these records (see the kit's rules on people); the casualty count and the Report of Casualties page
are cited instead.

## Outcome

On the first day of Japan's mass kamikaze offensive at Okinawa, a destroyer posted 12 miles out from Task Group
58.3's carriers was hit by a single dive bomber that came out of low cloud, [[step:s7|at 1250 Item]]. Seven men died
at once ([Report of Casualties, action report p. 22](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140065945?objectPage=22)); of the
twenty-seven wounded, five died of their burns aboard South Dakota by 9 April, bringing the dead to twelve ([action
report, p. 5](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140065945?objectPage=5)). The fire was under control in about ten
minutes; with her radio and radar gone she couldn't report, and the squadron logged the hit when word reached it at
1318 ([COMDESRON 62 war diary, p. 8](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140016153?objectPage=8)).

## The picket line

Fast carrier Task Group 58.3 (Essex, Bunker Hill, Hancock, Cabot, three battleships and their screen) was east of the
Ryukyus, launching strikes on Okinawa and the islands to its north. Three destroyers were posted 12 miles out from
the formation as radar pickets, to see raids coming and to put the fighters on them before they reached the carriers
([COMDESRON 62 war diary, p. 7](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140016153?objectPage=7)). [[step:s1|Haynsworth took
station #1, bearing 060° from the group, at 1700 on 5 April]] ([war diary, p.
3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=3)).

## Found

[[step:s2|At 0800 a Saiun scout of Reconnaissance Squadron 11 reported four carriers at メツ三ス]]
[[cite:jacar:C13120384700]]. The Japanese Navy gave positions as a kana code for a square 20 minutes of latitude by 20
of longitude, about 20 miles across. The squares on the map are our decoding of those codes from other Japanese
reports, not a Japanese chart. If the scout's navigation had been exact, the carriers it saw would be inside its
square. TG 58.3 was about 20 miles west of it at 0800 ([Haynsworth war diary, p.
3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=3)). So either the scout saw TG 58.3 and was 20-30 miles out,
which is ordinary for a plane navigating by dead reckoning over open sea, or it saw another of TF 58's carrier
groups. Either way, this square is where the morning's Suisei were sent [[cite:jacar:C13120082600]].

## Launch from Kyushu

[[step:s3|Fifth Air Fleet sent 30 bomb-carrying Zeros and 23 Suisei dive bombers (US code name Judy) off one after
another from 1015]] [[cite:jacar:C16120697000]]. The 210th Air Group's eleven Suisei left First Kokubu at 1020
[[cite:jacar:C13120082600]]. The straight course to their aiming point is about 300 miles: their actual route isn't
recorded, and none of the seven that didn't return left a track chart [[cite:jacar:C13120082800]].

## The fighters meet them

The task group's combat air patrol met the raids to the north and north-east, on Haynsworth's side of the formation.
[[step:s4|Essex's fighters shot down two Judys at 1140, 35 miles out on 035°, and another at 1159, 20 miles due
north]] ([Essex chronology, p. 34](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/101731703?objectPage=34)). The 210th's escort, over
Amami at 1140, fought Hellcats ten minutes later ([pico3298's transcription of the 210th's sortie,
secondary](https://ameblo.jp/pico3298/entry-10001925591.html)). At noon Haynsworth was at 26-55N 129-31E ([war diary,
p. 3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=3)). Between 1130 and 1420 the scouts reported four carrier
groups, two of them at メキ三ツ and スキ三ツ (the second a probable reading); TG 58.3 at noon was 25-40 miles south of
both [[cite:jacar:C13120384700 p13]] [[cite:jacar:C13120531300 p09]].

## Into the formation

[[step:s5|Seven Zekes were caught 15 miles north at 1201]] ([Essex chronology, p.
34](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/101731703?objectPage=34)). Under cover of that fight a Judy slipped into the
formation and dived on Cabot at 1226; its bomb missed by 100 yards and the plane fell off her bow ([Cabot war diary,
p. 12](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/139967685?objectPage=12)). Bunker Hill's Marine Corsairs came home from the
melee one short: 1st Lt. J. H. Carpenter was last seen diving on an enemy plane ([Bunker Hill war diary, p.
8](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633434642?objectPage=8)).

## Bogey at 22 miles

The 210th's report presumes all seven of its Suisei dived into the carrier force between 1230 and 1300
[[cite:jacar:C13120082700]]. [[step:s6|At 1232 Haynsworth's SC-3 radar had a bogey bearing 010°, 22 miles; she turned
to 020° and the fighters were vectored onto it]] ([war diary, p. 3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=3)).
At 1245 her crew heard a dog-fight and machine-gun fire to port, and a plane was splashed 1,500 yards off the port
quarter ([action report, p. 3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140065945?objectPage=3)). The map puts her where her
diary does, at her noon fix, 26-55N 129-31E, and plots the bogey from there. The picket station shown in the other
steps is where she was ordered to be, 12 miles 060° from the formation's centre, which no record gives; it lies about
9 miles from her fix, within the error of a dead-reckoned position.

## 1250 Item

[[step:s7|A Judy came out of the low cloud in a steep dive with a Corsair on either side]]. All three pulled out short
of the water, and the Judy flattened out and headed away. Haynsworth opened fire at 1,500 yards; the Judy turned to
starboard, winged over and came back. Its bomb fell 1,000 yards abeam. The plane struck at the twin 40 mm mount 41,
abaft the bridge, at 1250 ([action report, p. 3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140065945?objectPage=3); [war diary,
p. 3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=3)). The working's panels follow the report step by step,
and draw the plane beside the ship to scale.

## Fire

A gasoline fireball went through Main Radio, the 40 mm platform, CIC and plot. [[step:s8|Seven men were dead]]
([Report of Casualties, action report p. 22](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140065945?objectPage=22)). The fire was
under control in about ten minutes and out in about fifty-five ([war diary, p.
4](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=4)). With her radio and radar gone she couldn't report: the
squadron diary logs the hit at 1318 ([COMDESRON 62 war diary, p.
8](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140016153?objectPage=8)) and South Dakota at 1321, when word reached them ([South
Dakota war diary](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633438508)). Ten minutes after the hit, a suicide Zeke clipped
Cabot's radar ([Cabot war diary, p. 12](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/139967685?objectPage=12)), and a friendly
5-inch shell killed three men on North Carolina ([North Carolina war diary, p.
5](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/139988591?objectPage=5)).

## The afternoon

The raid on TG 58.3 was over by 1337 ([TG 58.3 report, p. 38](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140054760?objectPage=38)),
but the planes kept coming. The 252nd sent its second wave at 1555, and its crews who came down on Kikai-jima met
Grummans off Amami [[cite:jacar:C13120531300 p09]]; the 601st's four Suisei went out at 1645
[[cite:jacar:C13120018600]]. [[step:s9|The 210th sent two more Suisei at 1400]]. One plane's track chart survives: it
reached its farthest point at 1535, somewhere east-southeast of Amami and close to the task group, under rain cloud,
ten-tenths at 500-900 metres with 20 km visibility, saw nothing, and flew home past the Tokara islands
[[cite:jacar:C13120082800]]. The weather differed by place and hour, and three records give it: on the Japanese route
west of the islands that morning, ten-tenths cloud at 4,000 metres with heavy mist beneath and 10 km visibility (the
Izumi fighters, [[cite:jacar:C16120697000]]); over the task force, scattered to broken low cloud, the ceiling
generally unlimited, visibility unrestricted, northerly winds of 8 to 16 knots ([Bennington's war diary, p. 8, TG
58.1, a few miles east](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633421783?objectPage=8)), cloud enough for a Judy to come out
of it; and that afternoon's rain to the north-east. Haynsworth was at 27-23N 130-05E at 2000 ([war diary, p.
3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=3)).

## The day, from both sides

The clock under the map runs the day from both sides: play it, or step through it from the entries. The other ships'
positions are recorded only at 0800, 1200 and 2000, and drawn on straight lines between; Haynsworth's track runs
from her noon fix to the hit, which is drawn on her picket station, since her position at 1250 is not recorded. The
Japanese charts give the planes' routes but seldom their times. The 252nd Air Group's attack chart has two routes from
Kokubu: west of the Tokara islands to Yokoate-jima, then three legs fanning south before turning west into the
Okinawa anchorage, and east to Amami and back; its crews who came down on Kikai-jima met Grummans off Amami at 1430
[[cite:jacar:C13120531300 p09]]. The Izumi Zeros flew down the west side of the islands to Tokunoshima, saw an escort
carrier group and turned back without a fight [[cite:jacar:C16120697000]]. The carrier groups the T11 scouts reported
are placed from the war history's sketch to about 15 miles, in their hours (1130-1420 and 1505-1535)
[[cite:jacar:C13120384700 p13]]. Where a chart gives no times, a plane is taken to move at an assumed 150 knots from
its recorded take-off, and the chart doesn't say whether the 252nd's legs are the plan or the flight.

## The wounded, and the dead

Of the twenty-seven wounded, eleven went across to South Dakota for treatment on the 6th, and three more "who were
failing to respond to treatment" on the 7th; by the 9th five of them had died aboard her, bringing the dead to twelve
([action report, p. 5](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/140065945?objectPage=5)). Neither ship's diary gives the time
or place of the transfers: the two tracks on the map are the days they steamed together. On the 7th, with no radar of
her own, Haynsworth dropped astern with four other destroyers after Hancock was hit at 1210, and picked up eight of
her men blown into the water, four of them injured, while a pair of low-flying Zekes made a pass at the rescue group
([war diary, p. 4](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=4)). [[step:s10|The men killed outright were
buried at sea at 1816 that evening]], according to Irons' history, from the crew's accounts; no war diary or report
records it [[cite:irons]]. The burial mark is placed on her track between her 0800 and 2000 fixes, so its position is
ours, not a record's; her 1200 fix that day is typed some 55 miles west of the ships she was steaming with, so it is
left off the track.

## Afterwards

[[step:s11|On the 9th, while the group refuelled, Haynsworth, Hancock and Cabot were detached "to return to the rear
area for repairs and overhaul"]] ([South Dakota war diary, p. 8](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633438508?objectPage=8)).
At 1315 she joined Task Unit 58.3.10, the carriers in column 1,500 yards apart and Stembel and Haynsworth screening
ahead, Captain Smith in Cabot in tactical command ([war diary, p.
5](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=5)). Task Group 58.3 refuelled and stood north again, to give
direct air support to the troops fighting on Okinawa, where the landings had been made on 1 April. The unit entered
Ulithi lagoon at 1355 on the 11th; Haynsworth went alongside the tender Yosemite, and on the 17th sailed for Pearl
Harbor by way of Eniwetok, then Mare Island for repairs ([war diary, p.
5](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/633422068?objectPage=5)). The run to Ulithi is beyond the map's box, which is
drawn to the entries of 5-7 April.

## Clocks

All times are Item (zone −9), which is also Tokyo time: the US Navy and the Japanese Navy kept the same clock here, so
a 1250 in Haynsworth's report and a 1250 in a Japanese report are the same minute. Every mark on the map and every
entry links to the page it was read from. Anything placed by assumption is hatched or dotted and says so.
