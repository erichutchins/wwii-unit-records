Me 262 jets attack a formation of B-26s over the Danube at Neuburg, and three records that never mention each other
describe the same five minutes: the 106th AAA AW Battalion's history (its batteries fired on the jets), the 189th Field
Artillery Battalion's history (it watched from Bergen, just north-west of the town), and the 17th Bomb Group's three
Missing Air Crew Reports. The German side so far is secondary: Adolf Galland's last two claims, and published accounts of
JV 44's mission.

Clocks: the Army's B time and German summer time were both UTC+2. The MACRs' times are as typed.

Still to find: JV 44's own records and the Luftwaffe's loss returns (to name the jets lost); the 17th Bomb Group's
mission report (AFHRA); the escorting P-47 group's report; the 45th Division's G-2 periodic report for 26 April; the
106th's journal for the day (`ok45th-17541`, around p. 27).

## Outcome

Three B-26s of the 17th Bomb Group were lost to enemy aircraft over Neuburg between 1145 and 1152, one each from the
34th, 432nd and 37th Squadrons, each with its Missing Air Crew Report [[cite:rg92-91150810/p02]]
[[cite:rg92-91150796/p02]] [[cite:rg92-91150835/p02]]. The 106th AAA AW Battalion's history has its Batteries C and D
engaging six Me 262s from 1145B to 1147B "without result" [[cite:ok45th-17560/p06]]; the 189th Field Artillery
Battalion's, watching from Bergen, has four jets, three bombers shot down and "one of the German aircraft was downed"
[[cite:ok45th-14862/p10]]. Whose fire, if any, brought a jet down, no record read says. The German side is secondary so
far: [Galland's two claims that day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aerial_victories_claimed_by_Adolf_Galland),
and the published accounts of JV 44's mission.
