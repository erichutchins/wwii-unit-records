Two ships of convoy UGS-72, the Liberty ship Michael J. Stone and the British tanker Regent Lion, were torpedoed as
the convoy entered the Strait of Gibraltar on the morning of 17 February 1945. USS Solar, one of the escorts, logged
both as "struck by torpedo or mine" and took Michael J. Stone into Gibraltar Bay.

## Outcome

Solar's war diary for February 1945 records Regent Lion "struck by torpedo or mine, settling by the stern but not
sinking" at 1055, and Michael J. Stone the same at 1105, at 35°-57'N 5°-50'W, the convoy in four columns
([[cite:rg38-139905513/p03|war diary, p. 3]]). At 1121 Solar was sent to search under the escort division's
commander; at 1304 she was ordered to relieve USS Kearny (DD-432) and escort Michael J. Stone to Gibraltar, where she
arrived at 1545, leaving again at 1700 to overtake the convoy's Southern France section.

The Navy's summary of Michael J. Stone's survivors' statements, of 26 March 1945, has her "torpedoed at 0955 GMT, 17
Feb 45, in 35.55N - 05.51W", struck on the starboard side between holds 4 and 5, third ship in the fourth column of
about 86; the torpedo's track was not seen, "The sub was not sighted", and all 73 aboard survived
([[cite:marad:MichaelJStone_3359_002|Op-16-B-5]]).

The German Naval Staff's war diary for the day credits the sinking, from a radio-intelligence report, "probably" to
U 869 ([[cite:skl66/p183|vol. 66, p. 183]]). U-869's wreck lies off New Jersey, so that credit cannot stand. On 6
February the U-boat command's situation report has U-300 (Hein) asking for, and given, the Gibraltar operations area
([[cite:bdu-feb45/760|B.d.U. report 760]]), and the postwar accounts credit U-300 with the attack: two spreads of two
torpedoes, 27 miles from Gibraltar. U-300 was depth-charged by HMS Evadne and scuttled under the gunfire of HMS
Recruit and HMS Pincher west of Cádiz on 22 February 1945, with nine dead, among them her commander, and 41 taken
prisoner. No record of U-300's own for 17 February is entered here, and the Naval Staff's diary for the month does
not name her.

## The clocks

The survivors' summary is headed "ALL TIMES GMT": 0955. The Naval Staff's 1100 is German time, an hour ahead of GMT
(zone A, −1), so 1000 GMT, assumed. Solar's diary types no zone, and her track takes zone Z from her longitude
(assumed): her 1105 for Michael J. Stone is then seventy minutes after the survivors' time. In zone A it would be
1005 GMT, ten minutes after it, which points to her clock being an hour ahead of GMT entering the Strait; no record
says so, and her track keeps zone Z until one does.
