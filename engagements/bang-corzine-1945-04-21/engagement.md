Hit by flak over Ishigaki airfield on the afternoon of 21 April 1945, a CHENANGO Hellcat pilot spent fourteen hours
in his raft before BANG, sent from her lifeguard station off Formosa, took him aboard. BANG's report, his squadron's
action report and the Miyako garrison's telegram of the same day, on one GMT clock.

## Outcome

Shot down by medium flak over Ishigaki airfield and ditched about 20 miles east of the island; the other three
Hellcats saw him in his raft. BANG, ordered from her station northeast of Formosa at 1415, reached the reported
position at 2330 and, guided in by carrier planes the next morning, took him aboard at 0737 after fourteen hours in
the water. "Replacement clothing was no problem except for his size thirteen shoes."

## The target

Ishigaki airfield strike, USS CHENANGO (Air Group 25).

## Reading the map

BANG's track runs straight between her reported positions; her search southeast of Ishigaki on the night of the 21st
and the pickup itself have no position in her report, so the line doesn't show them. The dashed ring is where the
squadron's report puts Corzine's water landing, "about twenty miles East of the Island": an estimate, not a fix. Her
2200 radar-log position (125°14′E as typed) contradicts her own narrative and is drawn dashed.

## Clocks

BANG, CHENANGO's air group and the Miyako garrison all kept zone I (−9), Tokyo time, on 21–22 April 1945, so their
local times can be read side by side.

## The Japanese side

No Japanese record of Corzine's loss has been found. The Miyako-jima Guard Unit's action summary for the 21st (sent
2243) claims one plane shot down and two damaged over Miyako, with no time or place; it is shown for context and is
not his. Ishigaki's own report for the day is still missing.
