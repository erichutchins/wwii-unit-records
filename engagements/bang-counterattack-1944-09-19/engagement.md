The hunt after BANG's attacks on convoy TAKA-909: eleven and a half hours from her going deep to her surfacing, about
80 depth charges by her count, and the escorts' claim of a submarine sunk, set beside her depth log in the water she
was in.

## Outcome

BANG survived without recorded damage. Her report: "Very deliberate depth charging began 4 minutes after firing.
Three escorts ... let us have about 80 charges in patterns of 3 6 or 9 during the next 4½ hours. We were running
silent at 450 feet under a 24 degree negative gradient" (p. 7) [[cite:rg367-74802831/p07]]. The Japanese record:
CD-30 and Taian Maru, with aircraft, attacked the submarine at 24-54N 122-23E, "one enemy submarine sunk" (the 4th
Surface Escort Division's daily summary, p. 12) [[cite:jacar:C08030144100]].

## The hunt, from both sides

BANG fired at 0915 K and went straight to 400 feet, having seen an escort at about 2,000 yards with a zero angle on
the bow; she was at 450 feet by 0920 K. On the Japanese side, at 0938 I (0838 K) the 4th Surface Escort Division
ordered a 4th Sweep Unit, aircraft of the Okinawa Air Group and two of the convoy's escorts chosen by CD-30's captain,
"to destroy the enemy submarine that torpedoed No. 2 Tosei Maru" (W-15's diary, p. 57) [[cite:jacar:C08030607800]]:
the order came an hour after the escorts had begun. W-15 left the convoy off Keelung at 1256 I, reached the scene at
1625 I and joined CD-30; at 1630 I it received the report that CD-30, with aircraft, had certainly sunk the submarine,
and broke off at 1700 (p. 53). BANG came to periscope depth at 1730 K, the same minute as that report (1630 I), with
nothing in sight, and surfaced at 2050 K.

## Reading the figure

The water column is drawn to scale: BANG's silhouette at her logged depth, the Japanese pistols' depth settings as
War Damage Report 58 gives them, the layer from her bathythermograph card of that evening (isothermal to 120 ft, a
gradient to 450 ft), and the seafloor under her track. Where any charge burst is not recorded: the rings round each
setting are the Bureau's damage distances for 300 lb of TNT against the older, lighter-hulled boats, an illustration
of the scale, not a placement [[cite:wdr58:15/15-12]]. The deepest setting the Type 95 could take was 292 ft and the
deepest normally used on the Type 2 was 390 ft (paragraph 3-12) [[cite:wdr58:3/3-12]]: at 450 ft BANG was below both.
The explainer, *Depth charges*, works through the rest.

## Clocks

BANG kept zone K (−10); the Japanese records Tokyo time (I, −9).

## What is missing

CD-30's own diary or action report, with the charges it dropped and their settings: not on JACAR (searched 11-12 Nov
2026, with the Navy's telegram file of 1944-45). Which aircraft flew. Where any charge went off.
