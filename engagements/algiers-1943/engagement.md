Three nights when Battery C, 62nd Coast Artillery (AA), 4 miles west of Algiers, fired on raiders: 12 May, 13 May
and 4 June 1943. The battery's own reports beside the RAF night fighters' record book, the city's newspaper, the
Allied communiqués, and the attackers' side: the German Naval Staff's war diary and the Italian and German
communiqués.

Context, not participation: the records show the battery firing on these evenings, not what it hit. The German units
are not yet identified (the war diary gives numbers only).

## Outcome

Three nights, and the battery's reports show it firing on each: 2050 to 2125 on 12 May, 0820 and 2136 to 2228 on
13 May [[cite:rg64-427182819/p0519]], 2225 to 2235 on 4 June [[cite:rg64-427182819/p0537]]; they claim nothing hit.
No. 153 Squadron claimed one Ju 88 destroyed over the bay on 12 May and a probable on the 13th
[[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/37/p3]], and one Ju 88 destroyed 15 miles north of the city on 4 June
[[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/39/p1]]. The German Naval Staff's war diary reports the raids by number and names no unit: no
loss on the May nights [[cite:skl:45/p152]] [[cite:skl:45/p163]], two aircraft missing on 4 June [[cite:skl:46/p38]].
Whose fire the Ju 88 of 12 May was evading, the records don't say.

## Where the raiders came from

The German Naval Staff's war diary reports the raids on Algiers under Luftflotte 2 and by number only:
thirty bombers on 12 May [[cite:skl:45/p152]], 70 Ju 88s in its 13 May entry [[cite:skl:45/p163]], 19 bombers on 4
June [[cite:skl:46/p38]]. It names no units and no airfields. The Ju 88 Kampfgeschwader of Luftflotte 2 in Sardinia
and Italy are candidates, not identified: the records read don't say where these raids took off.

## The defences

[[step:s0|Battery C, 62nd Coast Artillery (AA), "arrived position 4 mi west of Algiers" at 1705 on 30 April]], after
270 miles from Châteaudun-du-Rhumel; through May its reports read "Usual camp duties 4 mi West of Algiers"
[[cite:rg64-427182819/p0503]]. The report gives a direction and a distance, not a grid, so the battery's mark is good
to about a mile. Its own reports don't name its guns; its sister batteries of the 1st Battalion do: Battery A, at
Algiers in the same raids, "went into action w/ 90mm Guns" on 12 May and "fired 453 rds 90mm" on the 13th
[[cite:rg64-427180737/p0633]], and Battery B at Oran "Fired 35 rounds of 90MM" in January [[cite:rg64-427181738/p0557]].
So the 90 mm is inferred, and its reach with it: a maximum horizontal range "as limited by fuze or tracer" of 12,600
yards, 7 miles ([FM 4-155 (1940), table A, p. 8](https://archive.org/details/FM_4_155_S_A_A_A_R_D_1940); FM 4-110
(1942) para 4 gives the same figure; a 3-inch gun would reach 11,100 yards), which puts the Ju 88's turn of 12 May
within it and the 4 June kill, 15 miles north of the city, beyond it. East of the city, No. 153 Squadron RAF flew Beaufighter night fighters from Maison Blanche, the
Algiers airfield, with AI radar, under ground-control stations known by their call-signs ("Mumsie", "Madbrain",
"Funless", "Perform") [[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/37/p1]].

## 12 May: one Ju 88 destroyed

[[step:m12|F/Sgt G.L. Linklater and Sgt L.V. Anthony went up on patrol in Beaufighter V.8632 at 1935]]
[[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/38/p2]]. In Algiers Bay they were told of bandits 40 miles to the north at 16,000 ft, then 5
miles away at 12,000 [[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/37/p2]]. Battery C "fired on hostile targets 2050 to 2125"
[[cite:rg64-427182819/p0519]]. The squadron's summary has the enemy aircraft, "taking evasive action from A.A.", turn
south about two miles west of Algiers; the Beaufighter closed, gave a two-second burst, and the Ju 88 "blew up and
fell into the sea" [[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/37/p3]]. The mark is where it turned, not where it fell. The record names
no battery: whose fire it was evading, and whether the two records' times are on one clock, stay open (see Questions and sources).
The German war diary has thirty bombers over Algiers that night, hitting the harbour, with no units or losses
[[cite:skl:45/p152]]. The next day's communiqué claimed at least three destroyed by the anti-aircraft guns and the
night fighters [[cite:gallica:bpt6k7587045k]]; L'Écho d'Alger called the D.C.A.'s reaction "très violente et
efficace" [[cite:gallica:bpt6k75870445]].

## 13 May: a probable

[[step:m13|At 0820 Battery C fired for two minutes, in daylight]] [[cite:rg64-427182819/p0519]]; nothing about it is
in the paper. That evening F/Lt S.S. Duff and Sgt R. Perfect patrolled from 1930 to 2230 in Beaufighter EL.162
[[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/38/p3]]. After a long chase they badly "mauled" a Ju 88 but didn't see it fall into the sea,
and were awarded a probable; where isn't recorded [[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/37/p3]]. The battery fired from 2136 to 2228
[[cite:rg64-427182819/p0519]]. The war diary's 13 May entry has 70 Ju 88s attacking Algiers and hits on six steamers:
most likely this night, possibly a fuller count of the 12th [[cite:skl:45/p163]]. L'Écho d'Alger reported a violent
barrage, and neither damage nor casualties [[cite:gallica:bpt6k7587045k]]. The Italian bulletins and German
communiqués printed on 14 and 15 May don't mention Algiers [[cite:lastampa:1943-05-15/p1]].

## 4 June: two missing

[[step:j4|W/O R.J. Smith and Sgt J.J. Sanderson scrambled from Maison Blanche at 2205 in Beaufighter V.8694]]
[[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/40/p1]]. Battery C fired from 2225 to 2235 [[cite:rg64-427182819/p0537]]. The squadron's
summary: "At long last the Hun has made an appearance", the crew's first, a Ju 88 15 miles north of Algiers
[[cite:tna:AIR 27/1027/39/p1]]. The war diary has 19 bombers attacking Algiers that evening, the target covered by a
smoke screen, and two planes missing [[cite:skl:46/p38]]. The next day the Italian bulletin and the German communiqué
said the port had been bombed [[cite:lastampa:1943-06-06/p1]]. The Beaufighter's claim accounts for one of the two
missing, if it is right; the records read don't say what became of the other.

## The morning after

[[step:after|L'Écho d'Alger of 5 June reported damage and some casualties]]; Admiral Muselier and the inspector general
of civil defence went to the damaged areas [[cite:gallica:bpt6k7587065b]]. The Allied communiqué, printed the next
day, gave a few enemy aircraft, some casualties, damage to civilian property and two bombers destroyed, the number the
German war diary has missing [[cite:gallica:bpt6k7587066r]]. On the Saturday morning Generals Giraud and Catroux
visited the bombed quarters, where clearing and rescue went on; Catroux called on Muslim families who had lost their
homes [[cite:gallica:bpt6k7587066r]]. Where in the city the bombs fell isn't in these records, so the map marks only
the harbour, the target the German and Italian reports name.

## Clocks

Times are as written. Neither the battery's reports nor the squadron's record book gives a time zone, so a 2050 in
one and a 2050 in the other may not be the same minute (one of the open questions under Questions and sources). Entries whose time isn't
recorded are placed by the order of the account and say so. The records show the battery firing on these evenings,
not what it hit. Every mark on the map and every entry links to the page it was read from; anything placed by
assumption is hatched or dashed and says so.

The night fighters' kills are placed as the record book gives them, by direction and distance from the city; statute
miles are converted to nautical, and the distances are the records' round figures. The questions the records leave
open: whose fire the Ju 88 was evading on 12 May; whether the times are on one clock; how many raiders were lost on
4 June; which German units flew.
