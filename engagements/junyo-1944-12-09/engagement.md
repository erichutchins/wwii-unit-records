Two boats on one carrier in the night of 8–9 December 1944: SEA DEVIL's four stern torpedoes at 0028 I and
REDFISH's attacks at 0156 and 0320 I, as typed on their attack sheets, beside the Navy's summary, which has Junyō
torpedoed once, two hits, at 0130.

## Outcome

Junyō, returning from Mako, was torpedoed about 50 miles south-west of Nomozaki: two hits, but she could make 13 knots
on one shaft and entered Sasebo about 1000 (p. 5) [[cite:jacar:C16120660300]]. The summary has one time, 0130 I, and
does not say whose torpedoes. SEA DEVIL's narrative times two explosions at 0031:40 and 0031:55 I, each checking with
a torpedo's run, and PLAICE signalled that she saw the hits: two hits, as the summary has, an hour before its time.
REDFISH claimed one hit at 0156 I, 26 minutes after it, about 4 nm from the summary's place against SEA DEVIL's 17 nm,
and one more at 0320 I nearer Nomozaki. Both boats' attacks are paired with the summary as probable or possible; which
made the hits is open until a record with Junyō's own times is found.

## Reading the map

Positions are each boat's own; lines between them are straight. Junyō is drawn from the boats' sightings, and no
Japanese position is given in figures.

## Clocks

SEA DEVIL's sheets type zone I; REDFISH's Attack 7 sheet types a J that her positions file reads as I (her
japanese.yml and claims.yml say why). Japanese records use Tokyo time (I, −9).

## What is missing

Junyō's own record and her escorts' December diaries: none found on JACAR by her name or the 21st Destroyer Division's.
SEA POACHER, PLAICE and PIRANHA were in the area that night (SEA DEVIL's ship contacts) and are not in the kit.
