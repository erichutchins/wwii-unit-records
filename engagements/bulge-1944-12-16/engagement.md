On the morning of 16 December 1944 the German attack in the Ardennes reaches the 9th Armored Division's
quiet sector on the Sauer, held by the 60th Armored Infantry Battalion; for five days the battalion and Combat Command
A hold the southern shoulder between Beaufort and Ermsdorf. On the 26th the command is sent west, and from the 27th to
3 January it fights beside the corridor just opened to Bastogne, at Sibret and Chenogne. Told here from the battalion's
own after action reports for December and January, as a transcription gives them, beside the German side as the Army's
official history tells it; no German record is read yet.

## Outcome

On the Sauer the battalion's three rifle companies were cut off on the second day: by darkness on 17 December the
Germans were in Beaufort, contact with the companies was lost, and at 1743 the headquarters withdrew to Savelborn
[[cite:eucmh60aib/1944-12]]. Their remnants came back through the German lines on the nights of 18-20 December, and
from the 19th the battalion held a line from Waldbillig to Ermsdorf, where it beat off attacks on the 21st and 23rd
[[cite:eucmh60aib/1944-12]]. Cole names what it faced as the 276th Volks Grenadier Division, whose commander was killed
during the attack, and puts the division's furthest advance at Waldbillig on the 20th
[[cite:cole/9/p232]] [[cite:cole/9/p236]].

Sent to the Bastogne corridor, Task Force Collins (the battalion, with tanks, tank destroyers and engineers attached)
took Sibret on 27 December, attacked toward Chenogne, and was driven back to the high ground north of Sibret by a German
counterattack that the battalion estimated at a brigade; it attacked again on 1 January, and was relieved by the 17th
Airborne Division on the 3rd [[cite:eucmh60aib/1944-12]] [[cite:eucmh60aib/1945-01]]. For the month of December the
report counts 1 officer and 63 men killed, 4 and 156 wounded and 4 and 141 missing [[cite:eucmh60aib/1944-12]].

The two accounts don't agree on the days at Chenogne. The battalion's report has it attacking the town on the 30th and
the German attack coming at 0530 on the 31st; Cole has its tanks in the village late on the 29th and the Fuehrer Begleit
Brigade's attack beginning at 0730 on the 30th [[cite:cole/24/p616]] [[cite:cole/24/p619]]. Which is
right needs the original report or a German record (Questions and sources).

## The clock

The transcription types no zone letters ("at 1743"). The times are taken as A (UTC+1), the letter the Army's reports in
this theatre type (III Corps' "1550A" at Remagen [[cite:iiicorps-aar-1945-03/p72]]), as the 12th Army Group's maps'
hour is: assumed, not typed. Cole's times are the US records' where
he gives them; his one German time, the 3d Panzer Grenadier Division's planned 0730 on the 30th, is German time, which
in winter was also UTC+1. Most entries carry only a day.

## Reading the map

Under the entries, the front and the divisions are the 12th Army Group's situation maps for 1200 on each day from 16
December to 3 January, traced [[cite:loc-12ag/ict21195]]: the army group's own units and its intelligence picture of
the Germans', each symbol in its unit's area, not at a position (`fronts/README.md`). The symbols are read on every
sheet of the window. They are divisions and above: the 9th Armored is one "9(-)" that the army group moves between its
parts (by Beaufort on the 18th, by Bastogne on 20-22 December, by Étalle and Paliseul from the 23rd), and Combat Command
A, at Sibret and Chenogne from the 27th, has no symbol of its own; the sheets can also run days behind a move
(`fronts/README.md`). The towns are drawn at their centres
(OpenStreetMap): the reports name villages, not grid references, except the command post's "9136" on the 19th, which
names no sheet and is not placed. The 60th's dashed line is its own stations, joined straight, to the day shown. The
relief is today's; the Sauer and the Our, too small for the river data used, show only as their valleys.

## Sources and what's missing

The battalion's reports are read in a transcription by the European Center of Military History, on a page that is now
gone (read through the Internet Archive); it has no page numbers and has not been checked against the original in the
National Archives (RG 407). It has at least one slip, "Dec 19" for the 27th. The German side is Hugh Cole's official
history, *The Ardennes* (1965), which drew on the German commanders' postwar manuscripts: it is secondary here until
those, or the war diaries of the 276th Volks Grenadier Division, LXXX Corps, the 26th Volks Grenadier Division and the
Fuehrer Begleit Brigade, are read.

Still to read: the 9th Armored Division's and Combat Command A's after action reports for December; the 19th Tank
Battalion's and the 89th Cavalry Reconnaissance Squadron's; the battalion's original report; and the German records.
