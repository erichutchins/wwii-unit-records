The fleet oiler Kazahaya, sailing alone for Truk, met two American submarines on 6 October 1943, each on its own
patrol. STEELHEAD hit her first, before dawn; TINOSA found her that morning and attacked twice, and she sank in the
evening. The boats' war patrol reports beside her own signals, kept in the 2nd Destroyer Squadron's diary, on one GMT
clock.

## Outcome

Kazahaya's signals: torpedoed in the early morning, hit again at 1105 (TINOSA's morning attack, three hits), the
boiler room torpedoed at 1727, and sunk at 1830 (Tokyo time); five hits in four engagements by her own count; the
captain and 122 rescued, and later counts of 154 and 98 by the two destroyers sent to her (jacar:C08030101300). JANAC
credits the sinking to TINOSA and STEELHEAD jointly.

## Reading the map

Positions are each boat's own dead reckoning and Kazahaya's signalled positions. STEELHEAD's report types her attack
at 10-26N 146-29.5E; Kazahaya's first signal puts her torpedoing at 10-30N 146-37E, about 8 nm off.

## Clocks

STEELHEAD kept zone K (−10) and TINOSA zone K in her narrative (her tables GCT); the Japanese records use Tokyo time
(I, −9). STEELHEAD fired at 0236 K, 0136 I: the Imperial Navy's summary has the first attack at 0140 I and Kazahaya's own
summary of her loss counts "from 0100", but the destroyer squadron's log has her torpedoed at 0244 I, 68 minutes
later, perhaps the time of the signal rather than the hit. Kazahaya's times for TINOSA's attacks match TINOSA's
narrative an hour apart, the difference between the zones.
