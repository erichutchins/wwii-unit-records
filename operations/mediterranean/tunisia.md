## What it meant

By May 1943, "with the southern side of the Sicilian straits in Allied possession, Allied surface vessels could
henceforth move with fair security through the central Mediterranean," and airfields along the African coast
"permitted long-range bombing missions deep into the enemy's European fortress." [[cite:howe-nwa/35/p676]]

The Axis losses in Tunisia, "added to those sustained by the Axis at Stalingrad a few months earlier, left the Axis
powers unable to take the initiative henceforth except for local operations." [[cite:howe-nwa/35/p676]]

For the U.S. Army, the operations in Northwest Africa "were of inestimable value in making it a far more efficient
fighting force." The official history doubts that the Allies, even with a bridgehead in Normandy in 1943, could then
have broken out of it. [[cite:howe-nwa/35/p677]]

> When the fighting ended, Allied Force Headquarters in Algiers was "a successful going concern ready for what the
> future might bring." General Bradley left for Algiers on 13 May for conferences on the Sicilian plans.
> [[cite:howe-nwa/35/p675]] [[cite:howe-nwa/34/p667]]
